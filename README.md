Fifty synthetic institutional specialists examine Mexico’s Securities Market Act, identify frictions, challenge proposals and assemble a preliminary twenty-article reform package. The educational value lies in the organisation of multidisciplinary drafting, adversarial review and traceable synthesis. The output remains a proposal for human legal review; the agents do not exercise legislative authority.

#### Example 12 — ESCAPES: an ecology of autonomous systems

The capstone begins with separate finance, engineering and cybersecurity societies, each with local expertise, memory and institutional rules. When a society reaches its competence frontier, it can seek outside expertise, form a relationship and negotiate a temporary compact. An “escape” is a bounded organisational crossing, not evasion of safety controls. The result is an ecology of locally governed societies linked by selective, accountable collaboration.

### Bonus capstone · Example 13 — The Artificial PhD Researcher

**Optional research capstone · End of Lecture III · Reflection after the course**

*Beyond assigning tasks: governing an autonomous scientific research mandate*

This final presentation invites students to reflect on a frontier architecture in which AI is not merely assigned a task. Instead, a human defines a **research mandate**, a **constitution** and the **harnesses** that constrain permissible tools, evidence, experiments and decisions. Within those boundaries, the system must decide which questions are worth pursuing and what would count as a meaningful contribution.

#### INNER LOOP · AI FOR SCIENCE — Experiment, measure, evaluate

Formulate a testable hypothesis; design and execute experiments; evaluate results against the mandate's standards; generate metrics; challenge the evidence; and iterate on the research answer.

#### OUTER LOOP · RESEARCH-AGENDA OPTIMIZATION — Question the question

Review whether the chosen research question, methods and proposed contribution remain pertinent. Revise the research agenda when the evidence suggests a more valuable direction, within constitutional limits.

**The demanding human role:** define the purpose, the boundaries of inquiry, the standards of evidence, the permissible degree of autonomy and the conditions for review. Human responsibility moves upstream—from directing individual tasks toward architecting and governing the research environment.

## Course project — Can you design a financial institution that builds itself?

**Optional · Not for grade · Michaelmas to Easter**

Imagine that you are entrusted with an endowment: substantial capital, a universe of investment opportunities, access to artificial intelligence, and everything you have learned across our course. **Your assignment is to conceive an entirely new financial institution.** Not merely a portfolio, a trading algorithm, or a collection of agents following predefined tasks.

Are you ready to become the architect of an autonomous enterprise?

This is an **optional, ungraded intellectual challenge** for you to think about, debate, experiment with, and elaborate in your own way between the end of Michaelmas and **when we meet again in the Easter term**. No finished implementation or formal submission is required. Bring your questions, insights, alternative designs—and the assumptions you are prepared to challenge.

### 01 · THE MANDATE — Invent the enterprise

What business should it pursue? How might it formulate strategy, select opportunities, allocate capital and define success within an institutional mandate?

### 02 · THE ARCHITECTURE — Let the institution evolve

How could intelligent systems organize specialist roles, learn from experiments, evaluate performance and propose changes to their own structure?

### 03 · THE CONSTITUTION — Govern its autonomy

What limits, audit trails, independent tests, human approvals and powers of suspension would keep experimentation and institutional change accountable?

**The central distinction:** autonomy is not the absence of governance. You are designing a constitutionally bounded, auditable and scientifically testable institution—not transferring ultimate authority from people to machines.

**A question to carry into Easter:** Can you propose a better architecture, a stronger constitution, or a more convincing method for testing whether the institution truly improves?

### Explore one proposed solution. Then challenge it.

These materials are a reference point, not a model answer. Study, question and modify them as far as your interests take you.

## How to use the materials

Begin with the essay and its companion slides. Then explore each example by moving from explanation to demonstration and, optionally, runnable experimentation.

- **1 · Read and reflect** — The opening essay introduces the governing question; the monograph develops architecture, use cases and limitations.
- **2 · Review and listen** — Lecture slides reinforce the conceptual narrative. Podcasts offer an additional audio route into the ideas.
- **3 · Experiment responsibly** — Open the notebook with Colab or download the IPYNB and use a private copy; follow credential and setup instructions.

Notebook experiments may require external model/API access. They are optional for the classroom discussion. Linked Drive files retain their original sharing permissions; sign-in or access requests may be necessary. Examples are educational prototypes, not approved live deployments.

## What you will take away

- **Understand** — Define the financial problem, evidence needs, system capabilities, uncertainty and architecture appropriate to the task.
- **Govern** — Examine delegation, validation, permissions, safety, independent review, accountability and institutional controls.
- **Implement with judgment** — Assess prototypes and proposals, challenge unsupported claims and work effectively with AI specialists.

**The lasting question:** What form of intelligence does this problem require, how should it be governed, and what responsibilities accompany its use?
