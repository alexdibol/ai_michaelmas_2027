Lecture I · Knowledge, reasoning and intelligent action

From Knowledge to Action

Application A · Knowledge and reasoning

01

Legal knowledge graph

Legal inquiry requires more than a plausible answer. This example connects authorities, facts and relationships in a navigable knowledge graph, allowing students to examine the evidence behind a conclusion. The practical lesson is to distinguish fluent explanation from a defensible chain of reasoning, with applications to compliance, regulatory research and due diligence.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast
02

Central-bank knowledge graph

Central-bank questions span institutional mandates, policy decisions and financial-stability relationships. A structured knowledge environment helps connect those elements and compare alternative paths of inquiry. The example develops an approach to institutional memory in which the route to an answer, its sources and its limitations remain visible.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast

Application B · Intelligent action

03

Self-driving and racing systems

A racing system makes the transition from explanation to action concrete. It must perceive changing conditions, estimate its state, select an action and learn from feedback while respecting constraints. Students examine the decision loop and supervisory intervention before transferring the same architectural questions to financial operations.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast
04

Autonomous traffic control

The air-traffic-control materials extend the problem from one autonomous actor to a coordinated system. Local improvements must be assessed against shared constraints and system-wide consequences. This example introduces coordination and feedback as foundations for understanding market monitoring, operational resilience and liquidity management.

Lecture II · Autonomy, authority and governed implementation

From Action to Institutional Responsibility

Explore self-driving autonomy in trading, investment banking and cybersecurity, then examine how a harness connects capability to institutional authority.

Application A · Self-driving financial environments

05

Self-driving algorithmic trading

An LLM-supported research loop compares trading architectures and evaluates candidate strategies as market conditions change. The model participates in research and selection, while validation and risk limits constrain what can proceed. The central question is how to test a proposal before allowing it to influence execution.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast
06

Dynamic investment-banking ecosystems

An M&A mandate becomes the organising problem for an ecosystem of specialist agents. Capabilities can be assembled into temporary research constellations and reorganised when evidence or requirements change. Students examine how a constitution, defined responsibilities and coordinated review can support adaptive investment-banking research.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast
07

Self-driving cybersecurity

Cybersecurity places the autonomous loop in an adversarial environment. The system must interpret threats and decide whether to respond, contain or escalate under explicit controls. The example highlights why speed alone is an inadequate objective: effective autonomy also requires bounded permissions, reliable oversight and a clear route to human intervention.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast

Application B · The Harness Ladder

08

The Harness Ladder

The Harness Ladder integrates six experiments into one course example: accounting, financial regulation, central-bank deliberation, trading, bank liquidity stress and syndicated credit. Together they examine reliability, authority, deliberation, action, adaptation and evidence. Controlled tools, deterministic checks, preserved dissent, permission gates and provenance turn model capability into a process that an institution can inspect and govern.

Application A · Governed swarms and discovery

09

50 AI Agents Go to Congress

Fifty synthetic institutional specialists examine Mexico’s Securities Market Act, identify frictions, challenge proposals and assemble a preliminary twenty-article reform package. The educational value lies in the organisation of multidisciplinary drafting, adversarial review and traceable synthesis. The output remains a proposal for human legal review; the agents do not exercise legislative authority.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast
Illustrative documents: Reform initiative · Proposed decree
10

Cross-science market-regime discovery

Fifty scientific perspectives explore hypotheses about latent financial market regimes, synthesise a testable architecture and subject it to empirical validation. The purpose is to strengthen investment research and asset-allocation reasoning. Competing state estimates, model disagreement, robustness tests and out-of-sample evaluation matter more than an attractive regime label.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast

Application B · Evolving architectures and capstone

11

Advanced agentic architectures and self-taught optimizers

The Guide frames this example around agents that help design how their work is organised. The available materials focus on a concrete modality: the Self-Taught Optimizer (STOP). An inner loop improves candidate solutions; an outer loop evaluates and revises the procedure producing them. Model weights remain fixed. Students distinguish task utility from improver meta-utility and ask what evidence would justify changing the surrounding scaffold.

MonographSlidesNotebookPodcast
12

ESCAPES: an ecology of autonomous systems

The capstone begins with separate finance, engineering and cybersecurity societies, each with local expertise, memory and institutional rules. When a society reaches its competence frontier, it can seek outside expertise, form a relationship and negotiate a temporary compact. An “escape” is a bounded organisational crossing, not evasion of safety controls. The result is an ecology of locally governed societies linked by selective, accountable collaboration.

A practical guide

How to use the materials

Begin with the essay and its companion slides. Then explore each example by moving from explanation to demonstration and, optionally, runnable experimentation.

1 · Read and reflect

The opening essay introduces the governing question; the monograph develops architecture, use cases and limitations.

2 · Review and listen

Lecture slides reinforce the conceptual narrative. Podcasts offer an additional audio route into the ideas.

3 · Experiment responsibly

Open the notebook with Colab or download the IPYNB and use a private copy; follow credential and setup instructions.

Notebook experiments may require external model/API access. They are optional for the classroom discussion. Linked Drive files retain their original sharing permissions; sign-in or access requests may be necessary. Examples are educational prototypes, not approved live deployments.
Professional formation

What you will take away

Understand

Define the financial problem, evidence needs, system capabilities, uncertainty and architecture appropriate to the task.

Govern

Examine delegation, validation, permissions, safety, independent review, accountability and institutional controls.

Implement with judgment

Assess prototypes and proposals, challenge unsupported claims and work effectively with AI specialists.

The lasting question: What form of intelligence does this problem require, how should it be governed, and what responsibilities accompany its use?
